Campus Customs — App Check

Three checks run against the live site, captured in a real browser while both servers were running. The chat replies below are genuine responses from the PydanticAI agent, not mock-ups.

1

The chat reports honest stock and price from the database

On the Champion Reverse Weave Crewneck page I asked the assistant "Do you have this in large? And what does it cost?" It answered "sold out in L… available in M and XL… the price is $58". The database has that product at L = 0, M = 12, XL = 12, with XS, S and XXL also at zero, priced at $58.00 — so every part of the answer is true.

What this proves

The assistant refused the sale it could not honour rather than guessing. It named the exact sizes left, quoted the real price, and the product card it returned shows M · XL — the same two sizes — because the card is built from the database row the tool read, not from the reply text.
2

A category question fills the page with product cards

From the home page I asked "What shirts do you have?" The agent searched the catalogue and four matching products appeared on the page under "From your chat", as full product cards with image, name, price and a short description — alongside compact versions inside the chat panel.

What this proves

The chat is not a dead end. A plain-language question becomes a real, browsable result set on the page, and each card opens that product's own detail page. The heading quotes the question back, so it is clear why those products are there.
3

Usability improvement from Problem 9 — filter by my size

On the Products page I set My size → XXL. The grid dropped from 102 items to 79, with the note "Showing only what is in stock in XXL — 23 items hidden." Hovering a card slides up its size run, with in-stock sizes filled and sold-out ones struck through.

What this proves

Both halves of the improvement work on real data. 24% of size rows in this catalogue are at zero, so a shopper who wears XXL would otherwise scroll 23 products they cannot buy. The filter removes them, and the size run on the card answers "is it in my size?" before the click rather than after it.
